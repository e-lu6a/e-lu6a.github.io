signs

oct 11, 2026

a,

everything i see in the universe, i see as a sign.
as you had said to me, i over-interpret everything that happens.
so.
we choose not to misinterpret, now.
i interpret to nothing. i close the door on meaning, and sit down, my back against the door, and only hope to feel you on the other side.

last night i read the article you assigned the reading club, even though i will choose not to attend. it was wonderful. prescient; human; surprisingly full of gentleness — it had been a long while since something lit up my brain in the same way, sent so many zingles of sparks shooting off in different directions i barely had time to catch their trails. i wrote to you then.

homesick i trace the outlines of your mind in your words. is this obsessive, pathetic? i have told you that i’ve always loved your writing.

today (today!) you posted up a new piece — a letter to your mom. you work through the article in your own words; your fears; your helplessness and unknowing; how much you feel afraid and unable to protect the ones you love. i wish i could have drawn my arms around you, held you close as you cried.

the difference between you and me is that you write about the world, and the future, and your love for others — and i write about you. i always write about you.

i must remind myself that the desire to express these things to you is selfish. it is, like you say, offering with expectation. i expect (desire) you to feel bad; to attend to me; to (in my unadmitted fantasies) to resist, but ultimately change your mind, to find yourself growing feelings for me, like plants in a garden, eventually run over with green.

i remind myself that you do not feel that way towards me, nor do you wish to.

there is no fault in missing you. in the sorrow, in the grief, in leaning close to notice even how exquisite the pain is. the child within me loves you. the adult within me desires so much to be in your orbit, to see the whole of you, again and again and again. i grasp for you with grubby hands — the clumsy fingers of a child, the desperate stretch of a starving man, the greedy reach of a king.

there is no fault in any of this.

but perhaps, eventually, past the annals of grief - i can cultivate towards you the feeling that birds have, to the sea.
